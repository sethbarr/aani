Leafcutter ants farm a fungus and can learn to avoid plants that harm it. Fifty years of those choices sit in the literature, never joined to chemistry. aani makes the join, keeps every source, and measures where the evidence runs out.

24 measured against our three target pathogens in comparable units 2,174 without one

Behavioural evidence and phytochemistry are both machine readable. Whole-organism antifungal measurements for plant natural products are, for practical purposes, not. So the compounds exist, the behaviour that points at them exists, and the measurement that would connect the two mostly does not. The rest of this page is how we got there and what we checked on the way.

“No comparable measurement” is not “never tested.” We swept ChEMBL for every fungal assay on these 2,198 compounds, resolving 835 assay organisms by lineage rather than by name. 141 compounds carry a measurement against the three target pathogens; only 91 of those 598 measurements are in units our frozen rules can compare, which is how 141 becomes 24. Across every fungus, 497 of 3,700 measurement rows carry a value in a convertible molar unit.

Nobody has tested any of this against the ants' own fungus

The same sweep returned zero. No assay in the retrieved set resolves to Leucoagaricus gongylophorus, to its synonyms, or to any documented attine cultivar. We cross-checked at the name level as well, because one cultivar anchor failed to resolve taxonomically: no cached assay carries an organism name in any cultivar genus, and none carries a description mentioning attine or leaf-cutting cultivation.

That is the fungus the behaviour is about. When a colony rejects a plant, this is the organism it is protecting, and it is the only fungus against which the hypothesis makes a direct prediction. Of 2,198 compounds drawn from the plants those colonies accept and reject, none has ever been measured against it.

The broader sweep shows these compounds are not neglected. 115 have been tested against other human fungal pathogens across 63 taxa, 96 against crop pathogens across 86 taxa, and 220 against other fungi — though 480 of that last group's measurements are on baker's yeast, a model organism rather than a pathogen. Twenty taxa carry both human and agricultural roles, Fusarium accounting for sixteen, so the tiers overlap and do not sum.

Absence from ChEMBL is not absence from the literature. Extract-level assays and uncurated papers are a separate gap this retrieval cannot see. A hand audit of twenty compounds found exactly that: nine papers matched on compound and pathogen but assayed the crude plant extract rather than the compound.

Why the missing organism is the commercially useful one

Every application we would pursue runs through the same untested measurement, which is why one experiment gates all three rather than each needing its own.

ApplicationWhat a cultivar assay gives it
Leafcutter ant controlThe cultivar is the control target, not a proxy for one. Colonies are killed by killing the garden, so a compound that inhibits Leucoagaricus is the product rather than a step toward it. This is the only application where a positive result is immediately the thing being sold.
Crop protectionThese compounds already have an agricultural track record: 96 of them carry measurements against crop pathogens across 86 taxa. What the cultivar assay adds is the selection logic — whether plant chemistry filtered by a farming insect over millions of years enriches for antifungal activity at all. That is the question the whole prior rests on, and it has never been asked of the organism doing the filtering.
Human antifungalsLeucoagaricus and Cryptococcus are both basidiomycetes, while most antifungal screening has run on ascomycetes such as Candida and Aspergillus. A cultivar readout is the closest available proxy for the phylum where the clinical gap is widest, and it is far cheaper to run than a clinical-isolate panel.

The sequencing follows from that. The cultivar assay is the cheapest experiment that can falsify the shared premise, it produces a directly saleable result in the nearest market, and it reads out on the phylum that matters for the furthest one. A negative result stops all three before any of them absorbs real capital, which is the property we want from a first experiment.

None of this claims activity. It says the measurement that would decide the question has not been made, and that the same measurement serves three applications at different distances from revenue. Ant deterrence is a clue rather than a proxy: in one 89-plant screen, two of twelve strongly deterrent extracts inhibited the fungus.

What a single experiment would settle

The compounds exist and the behavioural evidence pointing at them exists. What is missing is one measurement, and it is the same measurement for every application.

A positive result on the cultivar is the ant-control product itself, because colonies are killed by killing the garden. A positive result across the panel tests whether a farming insect's plant chemistry enriches for antifungal activity at all, which is the premise the crop-protection and human-medicine routes both rest on. A negative result stops all three before any of them absorbs real capital.

Why antifungals, and where this prior should bite

Fungal disease kills on the scale of malaria and tuberculosis and is treated with four drug classes. Azoles, echinocandins, polyenes and flucytosine are effectively the whole clinical arsenal, and resistance is eroding each of them. The WHO published its first fungal priority pathogen list in 2022 with Cryptococcus neoformans, Candida auris, Aspergillus fumigatus and Candida albicans in the critical group. The structural reason the pipeline is thin is that fungi are eukaryotes: most things that kill them have a human homologue to hit.

Agriculture faces the same shortage through a different door, and the two are connected. Azole fungicides used on crops select for azole resistance in environmental Aspergillus fumigatus, which then appears in patients who have never taken an azole. A compound class that works outside the existing modes of action is worth more to both fields than another azole would be to either.

The specific claim, and its limits

What a leafcutter colony selects against is harm to a live basidiomycete, in a humid high-inoculum garden, over evolutionary time. That is a narrow and unusual filter, and it predicts different things for different targets.

TargetWhat the prior does and does not say
Leucoagaricus, the ants' cultivarDirectly predicted. This is the organism the behaviour is about, and the only one where a positive result confirms the mechanism rather than extrapolating from it.
Cryptococcus neoformansThe nearest clinical relative. Both are Basidiomycota, while most antifungal screening has run on the ascomycete pathogens. Phylogenetic proximity is a reason to look, not evidence of activity.
Rhizoctonia, rusts and smutsThe crop pathogens where the prior should bite hardest, because they are basidiomycetes too. This is a testable prediction and we have not tested it.
Candida, Aspergillus, Botrytis, FusariumAscomycetes. The prior makes no phylum-based argument here. Activity would be welcome and would not follow from the selection story.

Stated plainly: this is a sourcing hypothesis about where to look for chemical novelty, and not a claim that any of these compounds works. Its strongest form is that a behaviour filtered by a farming insect may enrich for anti-basidiomycete chemistry that synthetic screening libraries do not contain, and the sharpest test of it is the one nobody has run.

The hard limit is selectivity, and nothing here addresses it. A compound that stops a fungus growing in a petri dish is not a drug, plant secondary metabolites are frequently cytotoxic, and eukaryotic selectivity is exactly the barrier that keeps the antifungal pipeline thin. Any cultivar or pathogen result would need a mammalian cytotoxicity readout beside it before it meant anything clinical. Our own data also argues for caution: in one 89-plant screen, two of twelve strongly deterrent extracts inhibited the fungus.

What the pipeline does

  1. Reads published behavioural observations and extracts each one with the sentence it came from.
  2. Resolves plant names to accepted taxonomy through GBIF.
  3. Attaches the compounds recorded for those plants from a checksum-verified LOTUS export.
  4. Retrieves whole-organism fungal assays for those compounds from ChEMBL and applies the frozen eligibility rules.

Every link keeps a source anchor. Every network response is cached by request hash, so the whole run replays with no API key.

What we measured about our own reader

We scored the extractor against one field study whose eleven-species results the authors state directly. Table 1 of that paper groups species under printed headings — acceptance, immediate rejection, habitat-related rejection — so the reference directions are the authors' own categories rather than our reading of their numbers. Six species carry a single consistent direction across that table and the paper's simultaneous-choice tests; those six are the primary denominator. Miconia argentea is grouped under immediate rejection in Table 1 but accepted in both habitats in the simultaneous tests, so we hold it with the four habitat-dependent species as context-dependent rather than counting it either way.

Four distinct faults, all of them the source text or its transport rather than the model misreading: each chunk lost track of which ant species the paper was about; the model could not copy a plus-minus sign and substituted a control character; the character it chose differed between runs; and the cached paper text was itself corrupt, so the model silently corrected it and strict quoting rejected it for being right. We rebuilt the validator once for each, in the order they surfaced.

Across four independent draws the validator never lost a detected reference pair. What varied was detection. A later experiment drew eight further samples under a frozen validator, and the seven that completed each detected all six species, so the weak draw looks like an outlier rather than a property of the schema. Single-span extraction produced fewer candidates and worse grounding with no detection gain, so the two-span design stays.

One paper, one panel, and the people building the rules knew the reference. The reference directions have been checked against Table 1 of the original and are the authors' printed groupings, not our inference from their numbers; what remains unverified is the semantic support for individual table rows and captions. Performance on unseen papers is unmeasured, and that is the next experiment rather than a claim.

Is the untested chemistry different, or just unlucky?

The obvious comparison says different: compounds with no eligible measurement sit at median Tanimoto 0.320 from the tested set, against 0.649 for classified ones. That comparison is circular. Compounds measured together come from the same chemical series, so anything in the reference set resembles it by construction — and compounds that were assayed but produced no usable number sit at 0.640, indistinguishable from the classified group.

The control that works is a permutation. Two thousand random subsets of the corpus, each matched to the tested set for size, leave the remaining compounds at median 0.400 with a 95% interval of 0.377 to 0.425. The real tested set leaves them at 0.320, and no draw in two thousand reaches that. Separately, 37 of 809 distinct scaffolds among the unmeasured compounds occur in the tested set at all.

Within this corpus, assay coverage has been non-random with respect to chemistry. What has been measured occupies a narrow region, and what has not is structurally unlike it.

The reference set is 98 compounds retrieved during this run, not every compound ChEMBL holds for these assays, and the saturation curve is still climbing at that size. Reference and query are drawn from the same corpus, so this measures coverage within it and not distance from antifungal chemistry generally. Expanding the reference could change the contrast. Candida auris is absent from the frozen inventory.

Three other animal systems

We specified three further coevolved systems in advance and ran each to a coverage funnel. Each stopped at a different stage, for a reason specific to that system.

SystemWhere it stopped
Honeybee propolisThe open-access corpus reports resin chemistry and colony-level collection, never a bee choosing one resin source over another. Nothing survived semantic review.
Monarch butterfliesSeeding the known primary sources recovered the infected-versus-uninfected comparison that a discovery query missed. The join then ends at the database: ChEMBL holds 16,322 functional assays across our three fungi and none at all for the monarch parasite.
Attine ant bacteriaA positive control with two known relations. One recovered; the other missed because the prompt named the modern producer genus and the 1999 paper uses its historical synonym. Taxonomy resolves under a scoped microbial rule.

The architecture transferred. The outcomes did not, and biological transfer remains untested. Knowing precisely where each one broke is more useful than a clean sweep would have been.

The counterexample we kept

In a selected 89-plant screen using one ant colony, twelve extracts were strongly deterrent and eleven inhibited the fungus. Two were both. Ant avoidance is a clue worth testing, and it is not a proxy for antifungal activity. Accepted plants can contain inhibitors and deterrent plants often do not. The pipeline surfaces that contrast rather than smoothing it, and it is the reason the next step is a measurement rather than a shortlist.

What we would test, and what stands in the way

The validation panel is ten previously reported materials with published cultivar results, chosen so that a single experiment discriminates between competing explanations rather than confirming one. It includes two reported inactives — clausarin as a matched-class null, and kolavenol as a compound that deters ants without inhibiting the fungus.

Checking any of this

Clone the repository and replay the run with no API key:

.venv/bin/python -m scripts.run_pipeline --offline

The request-hash cache and the LOTUS export are too large to ship, so the repository carries run manifests, request hashes and stage metrics instead. funnel.json and pipeline_report.md record every number on this page. The analysis plan and its thresholds are frozen at 98b5e09; each subsequent change is a separate dated amendment, and the frozen files are byte-compared on every run.

Not claimed

No novel molecule. No validated structure-activity predictor. No prospective improvement in hit rate. No customer, revenue or demonstrated willingness to pay. No biological experiment has been run; the laboratory work is a planning export with a water-only simulation behind it.

The prespecified biological question is unresolved. Reporting that, with the coverage account that explains it, is the result.
