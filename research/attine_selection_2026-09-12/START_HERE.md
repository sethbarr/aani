# Plants that may contain chemicals harmful to the attine fungus garden

Research collection, 12 September 2026. Prepared for the antifungal project using the Obsidian notes in `anty fungals` as a starting point.

**The strongest starting points are Piper holtonii, Aristolochia argentina, Hymenaea courbaril, Virola sebifera, and several cultivar-inhibiting Rutaceae compounds.** Include **Pterocaulon alopecuroides** even though its extract attracted ants: excluding accepted plants would discard a promising fungal inhibitor. Ant avoidance is a useful discovery signal, but it does not reliably identify either the affected organism or the active chemical.

## Start with these original experiments

The order below reflects the usefulness of the evidence chain and the specificity of the chemical lead. It is **not a cross-study potency ranking**: MIC, IC50, TLC inhibition zones, growth area, and whole-garden outcomes are different measurements.

| Plant | Why it belongs near the front of the queue | Original source / exact place to check |
|---|---|---|
| **Piper holtonii** | Selected after field non-foraging observations. Ethanolic leaf extract inhibited the cultivar; bioassay-guided isolation identified **dillapiole**. Reported IC50: extract **102 ppm**, compound **38 ppm**. | [Salazar et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10886-020-01170-w), [saved original](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10886-020-01170-w), PDF 2–5 / printed 669–672. IC95: extract 369 ppm, dillapiole 122 ppm. Five-week acid-PDA assay with cellulose/PDA controls; fitted values have no reported confidence intervals. |
| **Aristolochia argentina** | Aerial-part extract and **argentilactone** were active in both treated-leaf collection and fungal assays. Reported fungal MIC: extract **1.95 µg/mL**, compound **0.90 µg/mL**. | [Diaz Napal et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.indcrop.2015.07.001), [saved original](https://doi.org/10.1016/J.INDCROP.2015.07.001), Tables 1–2, printed pp.277–278 / PDF pp.3–4. |
| **Hymenaea courbaril** | Classic chemical explanation of avoidance: **caryophyllene epoxide** reproduced leaf repellency at natural concentrations and inhibited the attine cultivar. | [Hubbell, Wiemer & Adejare 1983](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00376846), [saved original](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00376846), PDF 3,5. At100 µg/mL,8/10 cultures visibly collapsed within 48 h; damage incidence is not percent growth inhibition. [Howard1988 Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01022531) independently tabulates day 7 ordinal scores; culture-lineage independence is unverified. |
| **Flourensia oolepis** | Complete rejection of treated material and extract MIC **7.80 µg/mL**. Useful unresolved chemical lead. TLC screening was only weakly positive; retain both endpoints. | Diaz Napal 2015, Tables 1–2. |
| **Pterocaulon alopecuroides** | Strong TLC activity and extract MIC **7.80 µg/mL**, despite **AFI −45**, indicating attraction in the collection assay. Valuable test of what avoidance-based selection misses. | Diaz Napal 2015, Tables 1–2. |
| **Virola sebifera** | Cultivar-inhibiting lignans: **(-)-kusunokinin** completely suppressed growth at **120 µg/mL** in the thesis assay. Ant acceptance varies with leaf age and trichomes. | [Bicalho 2011 thesis](https://repositorio.ufscar.br/handle/20.500.14289/6502), printed pp.133–135 / PDF 155–157; [related 2012 paper](https://doi.org/10.1155/2012/785424); [Carlos et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11829-017-9582-9), [saved paper](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11829-017-9582-9). |
| **Ricinus communis** | Both choice/growth observations and a chemical study support investigating leaves. Active fatty-acid-containing fractions left 20% growth at 0.5–1 mg/mL, but no individual acid was established as causal. Ricinine was ant-active; its fungal outcome is provisionally interpreted as null from defective prose and held out of training labels. | [Bigi et al. 2004](https://doi.org/10.1002/ps.892), [saved PDF 2–4](https://doi.org/10.1002/PS.892); [Arêdes 2018 thesis](https://uenf.br/posgraduacao/producao-vegetal/wp-content/uploads/sites/10/2019/02/TESE-final-An%C3%A1lia-Ar%C3%AAdes.pdf), PDF 55–61. |
| **Pilocarpus riedelianus** | Stem-derived **xanthyletin** caused **100% growth inhibition at 25 µg/mL**. A precise molecule/organ/cultivar lead, although natural avoidance is not established in that experiment. | [Godoy et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1590/S0103-50532005000400031), [saved original](https://doi.org/10.1590/S0103-50532005000400031), PDF 2–3, Table 1. |
| **Raulinoa echinata** | Stem/leaf furoquinolines inhibited the cultivar. **Kokusaginine** produced **100% inhibition at 100 µg/mL**, with substantially less activity at 50 µg/mL. | [Biavatti et al. 2002](https://doi.org/10.1590/S0103-50532002000100010), [saved original](https://doi.org/10.1590/S0103-50532002000100010), PDF 3, Table 1. |

**The 89-plant screen is especially relevant to your underlying hypothesis.** Of its 89 ethanol extracts, 11 were fungal-screen positive and 12 exceeded AFI70, the paper’s threshold for moderate/strong collection deterrence. Only two belonged to both groups. Calculated from the transcribed table, that is 2/12 fungal positives among deterrents versus 9/77 among the rest. This small, non-random set does not establish predictive enrichment; the screen used one laboratory ant colony. Keep it as a paired benchmark with its negative results, rather than a list containing only the successes. The underlying numerical table and page images are included.

## Additional chemical routes worth retaining

**Additional lignan and terpenoid evidence.** The supplied [Pagnocca1996 original](https://repositorio.unesp.br/entities/publication/44608eaa-b84f-4614-8e00-6f6fb9c5caf1) resolves plant–organ assignments: V. sebifera leaves supplied sesamin/eudesmin/lignans 5–6; unidentified Virola leaves supplied episesamin/epigalgravin/lignan 8; Otoba parvifolia fruits supplied philygenol. Otoba is now tentative because its isolated lignan produced only 20% inhibition. Episesamin and lignan 8 were inactive at 200 µg/mL. [Howard1988](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01022531) links the tested terpenoids to Melampodium and Vismia via earlier isolation work; these plant leads now have a directly checked assay source. Kolavenol remained inactive against the fungus despite deterring ants. See the [validation report](FULL_TEXT_VALIDATION.md) for the source discrepancies and modelling implications.

**Rutaceae coumarins and alkaloids.** Beyond the first-validation entries, the original Godoy assay supports **Citrus limonia roots** (suberosin and xanthoxyletin), **Adiscanthus fusciflorus roots** (isopimpinellin), and partial inhibition from **Picramnia teapensis bark** (umbelliferone). Xanthyletin and xanthoxyletin must remain separate compounds. Root activity cannot explain leaf avoidance without a demonstrated root-to-leaf chemical link. [Godoy et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1590/S0103-50532005000400031)

**Helietta puberula** yielded active maculine, dictamnine, kokusaginine and anthranilic acid. **Simarouba versicolor** yielded active canthinone alkaloids; its 4,5-dimethoxycanthin-6-one is particularly interesting because fungal and ant effects separated. Both papers have conflicting concentrations between methods and tables, so their exact potency remains unresolved. The contradictions are visible in the plant table. [Almeida et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.14295/BA.v2.0.53), [Peñaflor et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.14295/BA.v4.0.24)

**Portuguese fractionation work extends the list.** Godoy’s thesis reports active fractions from **Cedrela fissilis, Cipadessa fruticosa, Trichilia rubra, Conchocarpus longifolius** and **Protium heptaphyllum**, among others. Some strong results involve unresolved mixtures, which makes them candidates for identifying active compounds rather than a basis for assigning a known molecule. It also tests other microorganisms: those bacterial assays must not be confused with the cultivar tables. [Godoy 2003 original thesis](https://repositorio.unesp.br/server/api/core/bitstreams/26e7fe74-e052-4666-b364-2813ace6c22e/content)

**Spiranthera odoratissima** has more specific chemical evidence in Terezan’s thesis: branch extracts completely inhibited growth at **1000 µg/mL**, while leaf extracts inhibited only 20%. Branch-derived **dictamnine and gamma-fagarine** each gave complete inhibition at **50 µg/mL**, and **skimmianine** gave 80%. These were 30-day visual growth measurements, not MIC determinations. [Terezan 2007, PDF 49 and 137–138](https://repositorio.ufscar.br/server/api/core/bitstreams/4deaae0b-4c69-4081-ace3-35baa2d01880/content)

**Accepted and cultivated plants provide independent leads.** Sesame leaf fractions, papaya-derived carpaine/squalene, the TRAMIL preparations from Senna, garlic, cassava, onion and green tomato, and Capsicum leaf extracts all warrant comparison with avoidance-derived candidates. Papaya work also tested formulated extracts on nests. Their supplied full texts are now checked; see the latest validation report for exact materials, endpoints and source conflicts. These studies do not, by themselves, establish that ants naturally avoid these plants. [Ribeiro et al. 1998](https://doi.org/10.1590/S0301-80591998000300010), [Lobo-Echeverri et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1080/09670874.2019.1610812), [Boulogne et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1603/EC11313), [Capsicum study](https://doi.org/10.25100/socolen.v42i2.6684)

**Grasses supply a useful resistance comparison.** The cultivar-specific results for **Brachiaria brizantha ‘Marandú’, B. decumbens ‘Basilisk’ and B. humidicola ‘Pasto Humidicola’** connect plant resistance with fungal inhibition. A separate study supports the B. brizantha effect, while reporting neutral outcomes for several other grasses and a pH-dependent sugarcane effect. Retain cultivars and medium pH when comparing these studies. [Lapointe et al. 1996](https://doi.org/10.1093/jee/89.3.757), [Castellani et al. 2009 original university record](https://repositorio.unesp.br/entities/publication/387fbb1f-2733-4333-97cc-11e7605012f9)

**Recent work adds leads, not uniformly strong potency.** The 2024 fern study gives the clearest high-dose result for **Parapolystichum effusum**, with no activity from any tested extract at 100 µg/mL. The 2026 oil study adds **ginger, bottlebrush, Surinam cherry, weeping willow and black pepper**, but its concentrations describe a solution applied to the agar surface. They cannot be compared directly with a broth MIC. [Fern study](https://doi.org/10.1590/2175-7860202475023), [essential-oil study](https://doi.org/10.3390/insects17060645)

## Counterexamples and weak links that affect selection

| Observation | Interpretation for your plant shortlist |
|---|---|
| **Gaillardia megapotamica, Lantana grisebachii and Lithrea molleoides** deterred collection but did not inhibit the cultivar in the tested screen; MIC >500 µg/mL. | Chemical repellence can identify ant-active compounds without identifying fungal inhibitors. [Diaz Napal 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/J.INDCROP.2015.07.001) |
| **Syzygium cumini** was poorly collected but supported good fungal growth. | Poor collection can have a physical, nutritional, or sensory explanation. [Arêdes 2018, Fig.3](https://uenf.br/posgraduacao/producao-vegetal/wp-content/uploads/sites/10/2019/02/TESE-final-An%C3%A1lia-Ar%C3%AAdes.pdf) |
| **Gallesia integrifolia** produced lower growth numerically, but its Tukey letters overlap the control. | Keep as tentative, not statistically established cultivar inhibition. [Arêdes 2018, PDF 61](https://uenf.br/posgraduacao/producao-vegetal/wp-content/uploads/sites/10/2019/02/TESE-final-An%C3%A1lia-Ar%C3%AAdes.pdf) |
| **Platanus mexicana / Eriobotrya japonica** extracts reduced growth modestly; toughness and trichomes also differed. | Plant choice plus an extract result does not isolate the responsible chemical. The paper also has date and P-value inconsistencies. [Infante-Rodríguez et al.](https://doi.org/10.17129/botsci.3671) |
| **Croton floribundus / Zanthoxylum pohlianum** fractions could increase biomass while altering gongylidia. | Garden function and fungal biomass are different endpoints; solvent effects also matter. [Santos 2013](https://repositorio.ufla.br/handle/1/1614) |
| **Tithonia / Canavalia** forced diets reduced garden performance. | Whole-garden loss may follow poor nutrition, refusal, ant toxicity, or direct fungal harm. The experiment does not identify the mechanism. [Valderrama-Eslava et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1439-0418.2009.01421.x) |

Delayed rejection experiments using **cycloheximide-treated leaves** explain how colonies learn about unsuitable substrate; they do not establish natural antifungal chemistry in the leaf species used to deliver cycloheximide. Endophytes, their metabolites, leaf toughness, induced defenses, nutrient balance and fungal detoxification can also influence choice. Those mechanisms remain in the bibliography so they can be controlled or tested rather than folded into a single “avoided” label. [Herz et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1093/beheco/arn016), [Arenas & Roces 2017](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0171388), [endophyte–leaf chemistry study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.funeco.2013.12.009)

## Using your existing BCI material

Your local Wirth transcription compares plants in a one-hectare plot with material collected by one **Atta colombica** colony in 1993–94. It contains 85 taxa: 49 harvested, 33 not recorded as harvested, and three ambiguous genus-level matches. The separate [33-taxon file](local_wirth_33_nonharvest_leads.csv) preserves this set and its original local provenance.

Use those 33 taxa as **exploratory sampling candidates**, after checking availability, accessibility, abundance, leaf traits and the original book tables. Non-harvest is not equivalent to experimental rejection. In particular, the Virola entries were ambiguous genus-level matches in the local transcription; the newer lignan evidence should not silently convert that ambiguity into observed avoidance. The original table comparison was not independently revalidated during this search. [Wirth et al. book](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-662-05259-4)

The recovered [Howard 1987 supplement](https://doi.org/10.2307/1938455) is another useful starting dataset: it combines plant-level palatability with nonpolar extract deterrence, alkaloid/tannin indicators, nutrients, biomass and water content for 50 woody species. Its original values still need careful transcription from the scanned tables. [Publisher data record](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.3521624.v1)

## Validation order for this purpose

1. **Confirm the source-to-material match.** Resolve plant identity, voucher, organ, leaf age, season, extraction method and cultivar strain before treating a candidate as replicated.
2. **Reproduce a specific original endpoint.** Start with the strongest compound or extract leads and retain the original solvent controls. Record MIC, IC50, growth area and gongylidia separately; do not substitute one for another.
3. **Test the value of avoidance directly.** Include chemical deterrents with null fungal results and accepted plants with positive fungal results. The complete Diaz Napal table offers an initial benchmark for this comparison.
4. **Bridge cultured fungus to garden function.** Establish whether the measured inhibition survives the colony’s processing, dilution and detoxification, and whether it reduces cultivar function rather than just changing morphology or worker behavior.

The bundle deliberately leaves unknown chemistry and missing doses visible. A promising plant–fungus association is a lead for finding a chemical, not evidence that every compound reported from that plant causes the observed harm.
