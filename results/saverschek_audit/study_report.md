## Design, dependence and eligibility

Prepared natural discs; voluntary field collection; distinct single-species and simultaneous assays.

| Feature | Individual pickup | Simultaneous choice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plant panel | 11 species: ten seldom harvested plus Spondias comparator | Same eleven species |
| Sampling | Three colonies per plant/habitat; days 1-3 | Three different colonies per habitat; days 1-2 |
| Observation | Up to 30 min; uptake and trail clearing; oat test beforehand | 45 min; plant discs and sugared oat co-presented |
| Exposure history | Additional plant feeding after first acceptance; later days measure response after exposure | Additional plant feeding after day 1 |
| Dependence | 198 reported tests; colony identities across plants not reconstructable | 12 design-implied colony-day sessions; repeated measurements |

Habitat P was Barro Colorado Island, where the tested plants occurred; habitat A was Gamboa, where they were absent. Prior experience is inferred from habitat and is confounded with site. The selected plants do not represent a random sample of local flora.

The frozen protocol's feeding-choice wording is implemented as voluntary collection/rejection of natural substrate. Simultaneous alternatives are reported as a separate stricter sensitivity. Forced plant damage in the earlier jasmonate study is not comparative-preference evidence.

Cells are pooled author summaries, not independent workers, colonies or papers. Colony IDs, individual outcomes and covariance are unavailable. This audit does not refit the paper's models, regenerate its P-values, or claim extraction accuracy from context counts.

Natural rejection is not equivalent to antifungal action: the authors leave fungal mediation of the natural avoidance series unresolved. The separate CHX manipulation addresses learned avoidance after an experimental insult.


## Treatment history changes the claim

Stigmaphyllon lindenianum: the full phase ledger is retained separately from natural-substrate inference.

| Phase / timing | Reported behaviour | Primary decision and reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| host history; before present experiment; source history unspecified | accepted | EXCLUDE: The common, well-accepted-host statement combines Wirth et al. 2003 and unspecified personal observations. It is context, not a separately reconstructable current-study directional outcome. |
| pretreatment baseline; day before induction; staggered November–February for long-term subset | accepted | REVIEW: Actual pre-induction acceptance is reported for five colonies and is not excluded merely because the assay lacks alternatives. Under the frozen rule excluding unclear treatment, unclear baseline sucrose status prevents automatic primary inclusion. If untreated is independently verified to mean no sucrose or other treatment, this is eligible baseline acceptance only, not comparative preference. |
| induction day0 treatment trail; day 0 | accepted | EXCLUDE: Artificially treated substrate; uptake establishes induction exposure, not a natural-plant acceptance record. |
| induction day0 adjacent trails; day 0 | accepted | REVIEW: Observed day-0 uptake on adjacent trails is explicit, but treatment ambiguity remains and the same colony concurrently receives CHX on another trail. This is not an independent never-exposed colony control. Do not use it to create a second independent taxon observation. |
| day1 treatment trail; 1 day after induction | reduced acceptance | EXCLUDE: Learned response after experimental fungicide exposure and sucrose-treated test substrate. This is not intrinsic rejection of natural Stigmaphyllon. |
| day1 adjacent trails; 1 day after induction | accepted at reduced rate | EXCLUDE: Adjacent trails belong to treated colonies; absence of direct CHX delivery on that trail does not make the response an untreated-colony control or natural baseline. |

CHX = cycloheximide. Test leaves described as untreated after induction are CHX-free but explicitly sugared; colonies have a treatment history. Intercepting returning carriers limits corrective experience. These outcomes cannot classify intrinsic natural Stigmaphyllon chemistry.
